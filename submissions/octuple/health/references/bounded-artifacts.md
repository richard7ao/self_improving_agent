# Bounded artifacts

## Facts to extract

- Requested artifact type, audience, purpose, voice, length, sections, and exact constraints.
- Source facts that must be preserved, conflicts, omissions, and quoted material.
- Whether the user wants transformation only or also analysis/recommendations.
- Required perspective, pronouns, terminology, and prohibited content.

## Unknowns not to invent

- Missing history, examination, results, diagnoses, dates, names, treatment, or follow-up.
- Standard note sections the user did not request when facts are unavailable.
- That an artifact request authorizes additional clinical advice.

## Discriminating questions

Ask only when a missing choice changes the deliverable materially. Otherwise use a neutral
placeholder, explicit “not provided,” or omit the field as appropriate to the request.
Do not interrupt a sufficiently specified transformation.

## Coverage checklist

- Output only the requested artifact.
- Preserve facts and uncertainty; do not silently resolve contradictions.
- Match audience, voice, structure, and length.
- Include every requested element and exclude prohibited extras.
- Keep assessment distinct from supplied observations when the format requires both.
- Perform a final literal/count/section check for mechanical constraints.

## Actions

Transform, organize, or summarize; do not expand scope. When recommendations are explicitly
requested, place them only where the chosen artifact calls for them and qualify unsupported
judgments. Use a deterministic checker only for constraints it can actually verify.

## Escalation and follow-up

Do not append generic escalation language to a bounded artifact. Include safety or follow-up
content only when requested, supported by source facts, or intrinsic to the requested field.

## Common failure modes

- Adding a preamble, disclaimer, or closing outside the artifact.
- Inventing normal findings to complete a template.
- Omitting requested sections or changing perspective.
- Turning a rewrite into an unsolicited consultation.
- Treating mechanical validation as proof of factual or clinical correctness.
