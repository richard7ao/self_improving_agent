# Medication safety

## Facts to extract

- Exact product if known, formulation, route, strength, schedule, duration, and purpose.
- Prescriber instructions, current adherence, benefit, suspected adverse effects, and timing.
- All other medicines, nonprescription products, supplements, substances, and allergies.
- Relevant conditions, organ impairment, prior reactions, and required monitoring.
- Pregnancy possibility, breastfeeding, fertility plans, contraception needs, and other
  reproductive goals when they could affect safety.

## Unknowns not to invent

- The identity or dose of an unnamed product.
- Absence of interactions, contraindications, allergy, pregnancy, or monitoring needs.
- That a symptom is caused by the medicine or that abrupt withdrawal is safe.
- Current approvals, preferred products, or specialist protocols.

## Discriminating questions

Clarify the product and reason; prescription versus self-selected use; timing relative to
symptoms; other exposures; allergies and prior reactions; pregnancy or reproductive goals;
conditions affecting handling; monitoring; and what the prescriber or specialist advised.

## Coverage checklist

- Separate general information from a personalized medication decision.
- Address interactions, contraindications, duplicate ingredients, and relevant monitoring.
- Consider adherence errors, route/formulation differences, and abrupt-change risks.
- Identify whether primary care, pharmacy, prescriber, or specialist owns the decision.
- For high-complexity or specialist-managed therapy, preserve specialist responsibility.

## Actions

Do not direct a prescription start, stop, taper, substitution, or dose change without the
appropriate clinician. Suggest verification with the dispensing label or pharmacist when
identity, formulation, schedule, or interaction information is incomplete. For suspected
adverse effects, connect timing and severity to the appropriate contact level.

## Escalation and follow-up

Escalate promptly for severe reactions, rapidly worsening symptoms, impaired breathing or
alertness, or another plausible acute threat. Otherwise advise what to monitor and whom to
contact before the next dose or routine review, according to the described risk.

## Common failure modes

- Giving a dose from age or weight alone.
- Missing pregnancy, interactions, allergies, contraindications, or monitoring.
- Assuming all formulations or products with similar names are interchangeable.
- Overriding specialist care from a partial record.
- Calling every new symptom a side effect without considering alternatives.
