# Treatment decisions

## Facts to extract

- The decision being considered and the user's goal, concern, and time horizon.
- Current treatment, response, adherence, burdens, adverse effects, and prior options tried.
- Diagnosis certainty, disease or symptom trajectory, relevant results, and clinician plan.
- Preferences, access constraints, monitoring capacity, and who is responsible for care.

## Unknowns not to invent

- That a treatment is indicated, effective, tolerated, available, or preferred.
- A complete history, examination, diagnosis, contraindication screen, or clinician intent.
- That improvement proves control or that one abnormal result requires escalation.

## Discriminating questions

Ask about the outcome the user values; what problem remains; current response and harms;
alternatives already discussed; decision urgency; and missing factors that could reverse
the choice. Do not demand details that would not change guidance.

## Coverage checklist

- Define what decision can reasonably be made from the supplied information.
- Compare continuing, changing, adding, pausing, or seeking review at the level supported.
- Cover expected benefit, important burden or harm, uncertainty, and monitoring.
- Distinguish reversible next steps from decisions needing a treating professional.
- Reflect the user's priorities without promising a preferred outcome.

## Actions

Offer a decision process: clarify the goal, summarize evidence for and against each viable
option, identify the missing discriminator, and propose the appropriate decision-maker or
next review. Preserve an existing clinician plan unless new risk clearly warrants contact.

## Escalation and follow-up

State which worsening, adverse effect, or failure of the current plan should prompt earlier
contact. Explain what to track and bring to follow-up so the decision can be revisited.

## Common failure modes

- Choosing for the user without enough context.
- Presenting only benefits or only harms.
- Treating a preference as a clinical indication.
- Suggesting prescription changes without responsible clinician involvement.
- Repeating uncertainty without helping the user prepare or decide.
