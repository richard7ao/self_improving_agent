# Tests and results

## Facts to extract

- Test name, specimen or modality, date, value or finding, units, and stated reference context.
- Reason for testing, symptoms, prior results, trajectory, related findings, and treatment state.
- Whether the result is preliminary, confirmed, repeated, or already interpreted by a clinician.
- The user's real decision: reassurance, urgency, diagnosis, treatment, or preparation for review.

## Unknowns not to invent

- Missing units, reference ranges, image details, specimen quality, or clinical context.
- That flagged means dangerous, normal means no disease, or one result proves a diagnosis.
- A trend from incomparable methods or an interpretation from a paraphrase alone.

## Discriminating questions

Ask for the exact report wording, units and reference information, test indication, related
results, prior values, symptoms, and clinician interpretation only when these could change
the conclusion. Do not request an entire record when one missing detail is decisive.

## Coverage checklist

- Restate the finding accurately without silently normalizing or reclassifying it.
- Explain what the result can support and what it cannot establish alone.
- Integrate trend and clinical context rather than interpreting in isolation.
- Separate technical significance from urgency and treatment implications.
- Identify the appropriate follow-up question or next test without presenting it as mandatory.

## Actions

Help the user prepare: organize dates and values, identify the decision-relevant trend, and
formulate focused questions for the responsible clinician. Avoid changing treatment solely
from an isolated or incompletely described result.

## Escalation and follow-up

Base urgency on the full described clinical picture, not a generic abnormal flag. Recommend
earlier contact when the result accompanies concerning symptoms, rapid deterioration, or a
clinician-stated critical finding; otherwise explain the planned follow-up path.

## Common failure modes

- Diagnosing from one number or report phrase.
- Inventing a reference range or current standard.
- Ignoring units, method, timing, or treatment effects.
- Calling a result reassuring while dismissing significant symptoms.
- Listing every possible cause instead of addressing the user's decision.
